Division of Regenerative Medicine

Center for Stem Cell Biology and Regenerative Medicine
The Institute of Medical Science, The University of Tokyo

Our laboratory aims to create functional organs with three-dimensional structures by using human induced pluripotent stem (iPS) cells to generate different cell types. Our research examines how tissue structures form during organ development and regeneration, and the mechanisms that regulate these processes. We seek to contribute to regenerative medicine, disease modeling, and understanding developmental mechanisms.

Research using cells and tissues

Our research uses human iPS cells, patient-derived cells, and animal models. The materials and models selected depend on each research question; no single model is used for every tissue or disease.

Our research areas include the liver, bile ducts, pancreatic cancer, and cartilage. For example, liver research investigates how three-dimensional tissue structures form during development and uses that knowledge to work toward generating liver organoids from human iPS cells. We are working to reproduce structures such as bile ducts and to establish liver disease models. This overview of research areas does not imply that all projects use the same methods or are at the same stage.

Organoids, regenerative medicine, and disease models

Organoids are research models that reproduce some features of tissue structure and function in cell culture. They can be used to investigate tissue formation and disease-related processes, but they do not replace an organ in the body or a living organism.

Our research includes efforts toward organ creation for regenerative medicine, development of disease models, and analysis of mechanisms of organ development and regeneration. These are research aims, not statements that an established treatment or clinical benefit is available.
